Key formulas:
% change  = (New − Old) ÷ Old × 100
After x% increase  = Old × (1 + x/100)
After x% decrease  = Old × (1 − x/100)
Always divide by the original (base) value, not the new one.
Q1

A textbook costs $80. The bookstore offers a 15% discount. What is the sale price?

Sale price = $80 × (1 − 0.15) = $80 × 0.85 = $68. Or: 15% of $80 = $12, so $80 − $12 = $68.
Q2

A firm earned $2,500,000 in Q1 and $3,000,000 in Q2. By what percentage did profits grow?

% change = (3,000,000 − 2,500,000) / 2,500,000 × 100 = 500,000 / 2,500,000 × 100 = 20%. (16.7% is the mistake of dividing by the new value instead of the original.)
Q3

The CPI was 240 in 2020 and 264 in 2022. What was the cumulative inflation rate over this period?

Inflation = (264 − 240) / 240 × 100 = 24 / 240 × 100 = 10%. (9.1% is the error of dividing by the new CPI of 264 instead of the base of 240.)
Q4

A worker earns $18/hour and receives a 12.5% raise. What is their new hourly wage?

New wage = $18 × 1.125 = $20.25. The raise adds 12.5% of $18 = $2.25, so $18 + $2.25 = $20.25.
Q5

A company's revenue falls from $5 million to $4 million. A manager says: "Revenue dropped by 20%." Is the manager correct?

% change = (4M − 5M) / 5M × 100 = −1M / 5M × 100 = −20%. The manager is correct. (25% is the common error of dividing by the new value: 1M ÷ 4M = 25%.)

Key formulas (linear curves → triangles):
CS = ½ × Q* × (Max WTP − P*)
PS = ½ × Q* × (P* − Min WTS)
Total Surplus = CS + PS
DWL = ½ × (Q* − Qtraded) × (Pdemand − Psupply) at Qtraded

Scenario — Widget Market. The demand curve is P = 60 − Q and the supply curve is P = Q. Both are straight lines.

The highest any consumer will pay (at Q = 0) is $60. The lowest any producer will accept (at Q = 0) is $0. All five questions use this market.

Q1

What is the free-market equilibrium price and quantity?

Set demand equal to supply: 60 − Q = Q → 2Q = 60 → Q* = 30, P* = $30.
Q2

At the free-market equilibrium (P = $30, Q = 30), what is consumer surplus?

CS = ½ × Q* × (Max WTP − P*) = ½ × 30 × (60 − 30) = ½ × 30 × 30 = $450. This is the triangle below the demand curve and above the price line.
Q3

At the same free-market equilibrium, what is producer surplus?

PS = ½ × Q* × (P* − Min WTS) = ½ × 30 × (30 − 0) = ½ × 30 × 30 = $450. This is the triangle above the supply curve and below the price line.
Q4

A price ceiling of $20 is imposed (below the $30 equilibrium). Only 20 units are traded. What is the deadweight loss?

At Q = 20: Pdemand = 60 − 20 = $40; Psupply = $20.
DWL = ½ × (Q* − Qtraded) × (Pdemand − Psupply) = ½ × (30 − 20) × (40 − 20) = ½ × 10 × 20 = $100.
This is the value of the 10 units (20 to 30) that go unproduced because of the ceiling.
Q5

With the $20 price ceiling and 20 units traded, what is the new producer surplus?

PS = ½ × Qtraded × (Pceiling − Min WTS) = ½ × 20 × (20 − 0) = $200. This is less than the free-market PS of $450 — producers are hurt both by the lower price and by the smaller quantity sold.
